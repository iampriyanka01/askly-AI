# Global People & Policy Manual — Orion Digital Services, Inc.

This Manual is the property of Orion Digital Services, Inc. ("Orion"). All employees, contractors, and temporary staff must read, understand, and comply with the policies contained herein. Failure to comply may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination. This Manual applies globally to Orion’s ~800 employees across North America, Europe, and APAC, with regional addenda where local law requires deviations.

Last Updated: 2025-08-15
Policy Owner: Chief Human Resources Officer (CHRO)
Distribution: Global — HRIS portal (PeopleHub) and Confluence

---

## Company Overview

Orion Digital Services, Inc. is a technology services firm specializing in cloud transformation, data and AI platforms, and managed DevOps for mid-market and enterprise clients. Headquartered at 875 Mission Street, San Francisco, CA 94103, Orion operates delivery centers in Austin (US), Dublin (IE), Kraków (PL), and Singapore (SG). The company is organized into three global business units: Cloud & Infrastructure, Data & AI, and Digital Applications, supported by shared functions (HR, Finance, Legal, IT Security, Quality & Compliance, and Sales Operations).

Orion’s operating model emphasizes distributed, product-aligned squads. Each squad includes an Engagement Manager, Technical Lead, Delivery Manager, and cross-functional engineers. Supervisors must ensure adequate coverage for customer SLAs, including follow-the-sun support windows for critical production systems. Employees are required to maintain accurate time entries in the timekeeping system (Tempo) by Monday 10:00 local time for the previous week to support billing accuracy and forecasting.

The company maintains ISO 27001-aligned information security controls and SOC 2 Type II-audited processes across core systems. Orion’s culture prioritizes safety, integrity, client value, and inclusion. Our leadership principles require managers to model desired behaviors, communicate transparently, and enforce policy fairly and consistently across all regions. The policies below reflect these principles and are designed to meet legal obligations while enabling operational excellence.

Rationale: A centralized, detailed policy manual ensures consistent standards worldwide, limits legal and operational risk, and sets clear expectations for employees and supervisors. Implementation: HR publishes this manual in PeopleHub; supervisors must cascade updates in team meetings; employees acknowledge updates electronically within 10 business days.

## Employment and Hiring Policies

Orion hires on the basis of merit, qualifications, and business need. Decisions related to recruitment, selection, and promotion are made without regard to protected characteristics. The Talent Acquisition (TA) team leads end-to-end hiring in partnership with hiring managers. Supervisors must ensure role descriptions are accurate, essential duties are documented, and competencies are measurable and validated during selection.

Background Screening: All finalists undergo third‑party background checks (Sterling) appropriate to role sensitivity and local law. Standard checks include identity verification, seven-year criminal history (where lawful), highest education verification, and employment verification for the past five years. Finance, Security, and privileged IT roles additionally require credit checks and professional license verification where applicable. Offers are contingent upon satisfactory results and lawful work authorization.

Onboarding Workflow: TA initiates the pre‑boarding package via PeopleHub within two business days of offer acceptance. New hires complete tax, payroll, and right‑to‑work documentation; IT creates accounts (Okta SSO, email, VPN) and provisions devices 5 business days pre‑start; managers submit a 30‑60‑90 day plan aligned to role outcomes. Employees are required to complete mandatory training (Code of Conduct, Information Security, Anti‑Harassment, and Privacy) within 14 days of start. Supervisors must ensure equipment receipt is logged and access is reviewed at day 30.

Internal Mobility: Employees may apply for posted roles after six months in role with manager notification. HR ensures salary parity and pay band alignment upon transfer. Employees on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) are typically ineligible for transfer until satisfactory progress is documented.

Rationale: Robust screening and structured onboarding reduce operational risk, improve time‑to‑productivity, and support regulatory obligations. Implementation: HRIS workflows gate progress; TA and managers receive automated SLA alerts; audits are performed quarterly by HR Operations.

## Compensation and Benefits

Compensation Philosophy: Orion aims to pay at the market median (50th–60th percentile) for fully proficient performance, with upside for high impact and critical skills. Pay ranges are published internally by job family and level. Supervisors must ensure pay decisions are documented against skills, scope, and performance criteria to maintain internal equity and legal compliance.

Salary Bands (illustrative US ranges; regional bands vary by market index):
- Software Engineer II: $105,000 – $145,000 base; target bonus 7%
- Senior Data Engineer: $135,000 – $185,000 base; target bonus 10%
- Site Reliability Engineer: $120,000 – $165,000 base; target bonus 8%
- Engagement Manager: $130,000 – $180,000 base; target bonus 12%
- Director, Delivery: $175,000 – $240,000 base; target bonus 15%

Benefits (US reference; regional equivalents provided in local addenda): Company pays 75% of employee medical premiums and 60% of dependent premiums; dental 50% employee/40% dependents; vision 100% employee/50% dependents. 401(k) match at 4% of eligible compensation; HSA match up to $800/year; life insurance at 1x base salary (buy‑up available). Educational assistance up to $3,000/year for approved programs. Wellness stipend of $600/year. Employees are eligible the first of the month following 30 days of employment unless more generous local law applies.

Annual Review Cycle: Base salary reviews occur in March based on performance ratings and market movement. Bonus payouts occur in February for the prior fiscal year; employees must be active and in good standing at payout. Salary increases for promotions are effective at the start of the next pay period following approval. HR audits comp changes for out‑of‑range exceptions.

Rationale: Transparent, market‑aligned compensation and competitive benefits support attraction, retention, and fairness. Implementation: PeopleHub comp module enforces band checks; Finance reviews budget impact; managers provide rationale notes for all changes.

## Workplace Standards and Remote Work

Standard Hours: Orion’s standard workweek is Monday–Friday, 40 hours. Typical core hours are 10:00–16:00 local, with flexibility to start between 7:00–10:00 and finish between 16:00–19:00, subject to client needs. Overtime for non‑exempt employees must be pre‑approved by supervisors and is paid in accordance with local law. Non‑exempt employees are required to record all hours worked; off‑the‑clock work is prohibited.

Remote & Hybrid Work: Orion operates a hybrid model. Office‑aligned roles are expected onsite two days per week; client‑embedded roles follow client protocols; fully remote roles require VP approval. Employees must maintain a safe, private, and ergonomic workspace, use company‑managed devices, and comply with IT security controls (e.g., disk encryption, MDM). Supervisors must ensure team coverage across time zones and maintain meeting practices inclusive of remote participants.

Workplace Conduct: Employees are required to maintain professional decorum in all interactions, including digital communications. Harassment, bullying, or discriminatory conduct is prohibited. Meetings should be scheduled with reasonable notice and within core hours where possible. Consumption of alcohol or controlled substances during work is prohibited, except at sanctioned events with approval, and never when operating equipment or supporting production.

Rationale: Clear standards enable productivity and equitable treatment across distributed teams while managing compliance risk. Implementation: Managers document hybrid schedules in team charters; HR performs quarterly spot checks on timekeeping and break compliance; Facilities conducts periodic ergonomic clinics onsite and virtual.

## Code of Conduct

Ethics & Integrity: Employees are required to act with honesty, avoid conflicts of interest, and protect confidential information. Gifts or hospitality exceeding $150 USD (or local equivalent) require Compliance approval; employees must not offer or accept bribes, kickbacks, or facilitation payments. Supervisors must ensure third‑party engagements comply with anti‑corruption laws (FCPA, UK Bribery Act).

Respectful Workplace: Unlawful harassment and discrimination are strictly prohibited. Examples of unacceptable behavior include derogatory jokes, sexual advances, intimidation, and repeated unwanted contact in collaboration tools. Acceptable behavior includes respectful feedback, constructive disagreement, and inclusive language. Reports may be made to HR, a manager, or anonymously via the EthicsLine (available 24/7).

Confidentiality & Insider Information: Employees must safeguard client data, source code, trade secrets, and non‑public financial information. Do not discuss sensitive matters in public spaces or on unsecured channels. Side projects must not use Orion IP or time. Employees with access to material non‑public information (MNPI) are prohibited from trading securities of Orion or clients.

Rationale: A strong conduct framework protects our people, clients, and reputation. Implementation: Annual compliance training; managers reinforce expectations in performance reviews; substantiated violations trigger corrective action up to and including termination.

## IT Security and Data Protection

Access & Authentication: All accounts are provisioned via Okta SSO with MFA. Passwords must be at least 14 characters, unique per system, and changed if compromise is suspected. Service accounts require documented ownership and rotation every 90 days. Employees are required to lock screens when away and never share credentials.

Device & Network Security: Company devices must use full‑disk encryption, MDM enrollment, and up‑to‑date EDR (CrowdStrike). VPN (GlobalProtect) is required for off‑network access. Only the "Orion‑Secure" SSID or wired connections may be used onsite. Personal devices may only access email via approved mobile apps with MAM policies; local data storage is prohibited.

Data Handling & Privacy: Data is classified as Public, Internal, Confidential, or Restricted. Confidential and Restricted data must be encrypted in transit (TLS 1.2+) and at rest. Use approved repositories (GitHub Enterprise, SharePoint, Snowflake) and disable public links by default. Privacy by Design is required in projects processing personal data; consult the Privacy Office for DPIAs in the EU.

Incident Response: Suspected incidents (e.g., phishing, malware alerts, lost device, unauthorized access) must be reported within 1 hour to security@orionds.com or via the Security Console. The Security team will triage, contain, eradicate, and recover per the IR playbooks, and Legal will assess notification obligations. Failure to report promptly may result in disciplinary action.

Rationale: Strong controls reduce breach risk and support ISO 27001 and SOC 2 obligations. Implementation: Quarterly access reviews; automated CIS benchmark checks; annual tabletop exercises with IT, Legal, and HR.

## Health & Safety

Safety Management: Orion maintains a safety program aligned to OSHA (US), HSA (IE), PUWER (UK/EU), and WSH (SG) standards. Employees are required to complete annual safety training covering ergonomic practices, electrical safety for IT equipment, and emergency procedures. All incidents and near misses must be reported in PeopleHub within 24 hours.

Ergonomics & Wellbeing: Facilities provides adjustable seating and monitors in offices; remote employees may expense up to $400 every two years for ergonomic equipment with manager approval. Supervisors must ensure regular breaks are observed and workloads are balanced to reduce burnout risk. The Employee Assistance Program (EAP) offers confidential counseling and wellbeing resources.

Emergency Procedures: Office evacuation maps are posted; conduct drills at least annually. In the event of fire, evacuate immediately and assemble at the designated point. For medical emergencies, call local emergency services, notify Facilities, and do not move injured persons unless necessary for safety. For severe weather or civil disruptions, follow local authority instructions and the Business Continuity Plan.

Rationale: A safe workplace mitigates risk and supports productivity. Implementation: Quarterly safety inspections; ergonomic assessments upon request; incident trend reviews in the QBR.

## Leave and Time‑Off Policies

Vacation (PTO): Employees accrue 1.25 days per month (15 days annually) during years 0–2; 1.67 days per month (20 days annually) during years 3–5; and 2.08 days per month (25 days annually) after year 6. Carryover up to 5 days is permitted unless local law is more generous. PTO must be requested via PeopleHub at least 10 calendar days in advance for absences of three days or more. Supervisors must ensure adequate coverage and cannot unreasonably deny requests.

Sick Leave: Employees receive 10 paid sick days per year, accrued monthly. Use is permitted for personal illness, medical appointments, or to care for an ill family member. Documentation may be required for absences of three consecutive days. Employees must notify their manager as soon as practicable and record time in PeopleHub.

Family & Other Leave: Parental leave provides 12 weeks paid for birthing parents and 6 weeks paid for non‑birthing parents, coordinated with applicable local benefits. Bereavement leave provides up to 3 paid days for immediate family and 1 day for extended family. Jury duty is paid in accordance with local law. Military leave and civic duty leave follow statutory requirements in each jurisdiction.

Examples: If an employee needs surgery with a two‑week recovery, they should request PTO or applicable medical leave as soon as the date is scheduled, attach supporting documentation, and coordinate a handover plan. If a child wakes ill, the employee may use sick leave and notify the manager before core hours.

Rationale: Predictable and equitable leave supports wellbeing and business continuity. Implementation: HR monitors leave balances; managers approve/deny within two business days; exceptions require HR Business Partner approval.

## Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures

Progressive Discipline: Orion applies a fair, consistent process: (1) Coaching/Verbal Warning; (2) Written Warning; (3) Final Written Warning; (4) Termination. Steps may be skipped for severe misconduct (e.g., violence, harassment, data theft, fraud). Supervisors must consult HR before issuing written discipline. All actions are documented in the HRIS.

Performance Improvement Plans (PIP): For sustained underperformance, managers may implement a 60–90 day PIP with clear objectives, resources, and review checkpoints. Employees are required to attend weekly check‑ins; failure to demonstrate progress may result in reassignment or separation.

Grievance Process: Employees should raise concerns with their manager or HR. Formal grievances are submitted via PeopleHub and will be acknowledged within 3 business days. HR will investigate impartially, typically within 15 business days, including interviews and review of relevant records. Retaliation against individuals who raise concerns in good faith is strictly prohibited and will result in disciplinary action.

Example: If an employee reports repeated derogatory comments in a team channel, HR will secure logs, interview parties and witnesses, evaluate policy violations, and recommend corrective action, which may include training, discipline, or termination.

Rationale: Structured processes ensure due process and respect for all parties. Implementation: HR maintains a case log, tracks SLAs, and reviews outcomes quarterly for consistency.

## Policy Administration

Ownership & Governance: The CHRO owns this Manual; functional owners (e.g., CISO, General Counsel) own domain policies. The Policy Review Board (PRB) meets semi‑annually to review changes, assess audit findings, and approve updates. Local addenda are maintained by Regional HR to reflect legal requirements.

Update Cycle: Policies are reviewed at least annually or upon material changes in law or business operations. Emergency updates may be issued by the CHRO with immediate effect. Version history is maintained in Confluence; superseded versions are archived for seven years.

Acknowledgment & Access: Employees must acknowledge this Manual and all updates in PeopleHub within 10 business days. Supervisors must ensure new hires complete acknowledgments during onboarding. The authoritative source of truth is the version published in PeopleHub; printed copies are uncontrolled.

Exceptions: Exception requests must document business justification, risk assessment, and compensating controls, and require approval from the policy owner and Risk & Compliance.
